NNGen is a diffusion model with a text-to-image architecture — except the “image” it denoises is the weight tensor of a working neural network. Describe a behavior in English, get a brand-new network that performs it.

Try the demo →

How it works

Training pairs mirror the text-to-image recipe — (image, caption) becomes (network, caption):

  • The image is a 6×32×32 weight canvas: the six parameter tensors of a small fixed-size controller MLP (8→32→32→8, ~1.6k parameters), laid out like image channels.
  • The caption is an English description of the behavior (“a tiny MLP that drives a pen to trace a five-pointed star”).
  • The corpus is synthesized: thousands of controllers are trained through differentiable mini-environments for games and animation tasks, filtered by held-out rollout success, and captioned from templates. No human-labeled data.

A UNet with text cross-attention (v-prediction, cosine schedule, DDIM sampling, classifier-free guidance) learns to denoise weight canvases conditioned on the description — exactly how text-to-image models work, one level of abstraction higher.

What you get

Sampling from a prompt yields a brand-new network that is loaded and executed directly in the target environment:

  • The network — a real model, exported as safetensors, ~1.6k parameters, runs anywhere (CPU, microcontroller, browser).
  • The behavior — a rollout GIF of the generated network acting in its environment, plus quality metrics.
  • The weights, visualized — a heatmap of the generated weight canvas.

The demo focuses on drawing behaviors: networks that drive a virtual pen to trace geometric shapes. Training shapes include circles, squares, triangles, pentagons, stars, spirals, lines and arcs — and two shapes are deliberately held out (hexagon, octagon) to test whether the model generalizes to shapes it has never seen.

The novel twist: diffusion, but for weight matrices

Diffusion models power today's image generators. Their core trick is humble: learn to remove noise from a matrix of numbers, step by step, guided by a text description. NNGen observes that a neural network is a matrix of numbers — so the very same machinery, unchanged, can denoise weights instead of pixels.

Nothing about the diffusion process knows it is generating “intelligence” — it only ever sees matrices. Yet because the training corpus pairs each matrix with a description of what it accomplishes, sampling from noise yields a functioning behavior. That is the step beyond generative media: from pictures of things, to the things themselves.

Why it matters

Today, getting a small neural network for a specific behavior means collecting data, training, and validating — specialist work per network. NNGen moves that cost from human effort to compute: the training corpus is self-generated, and inference (generating a new network from text) runs in seconds on a CPU.

The long-term vision: a queryable catalog of tiny models — “give me a network that does X in under 2k parameters” — for games, animation, UI agents and embedded devices.

Honest limits

  • This is a research prototype: small networks, simple behaviors, one fixed architecture per generator.
  • The model can only generate behaviors resembling its training corpus — and, in the best case, interpolate between them (that is what the held-out hexagon test measures).
  • Quality depends on corpus size; a GPU multiplies both by an order of magnitude.
